# Redaction Research And Planning

For each confirmed topic, define reader intent, differentiated angle, working title, primary keyword, 5–8 section outline, and format-appropriate depth. Blog and editorial pieces normally target 1,500–2,500 words; informational guides 2,000–3,500. Treat these as editorial ranges, not filler quotas.

When no topic is supplied, derive candidates from audience problems, product truth, and author expertise. Research enough to distinguish viable angles, then ask only if the choice materially changes the outcome.

Research in the content language and English where technical depth benefits. Prefer official documentation, primary studies, public datasets, original repositories, and named real cases. Verify current statistics, framework/API versions, market or legal claims, and competing coverage. Capture URLs and claim support before drafting; use at least five unique credible sources for an evidence-heavy article, or record why a narrower source set is proportionate.

Never turn competitor prose into a template. Record what existing coverage handles well, what it misses, and the original contribution this piece will make. For technical examples, validate the actual current API or code surface. If evidence cannot support a planned assertion, remove or qualify the assertion rather than inventing support.
